Case Study 1: Spark the Engagement

Scenario: Imagine you're working on an app designed to help people build meaningful micro-moments of
reflection and emotional awareness throughout their day. The app already has a journaling feature and guided
prompts to help users process what they wrote. However, engagement is inconsistent...some users log in daily,
but others only show up sporadically.

Task: Design "Spark", a lightweight feature that provides a compelling reason for users to check in every day. The
goal is to create something that integrates seamlessly with users' routines, sparks curiosity, and engenders
emotional engagement without feeling like a chore or an intrusive notification.

As the developer behind this feature, you'll need to solve some key challenges:

-   Users should be able to engage in under a minute.

-   It shouldn't feel stale over time - you want meaningful engagement for your users.

-   What would this look like? What would be most effective for the user experience you imagine?

Case Study 2: TrendForge's Community Pulse

Scenario: Imagine you're an engineer at TrendForge, a growing tech community platform where users discuss
emerging technologies, AI, and programming languages. The platform has 10,000 active users and is steadily
growing.

Task: Design Community Pulse, a feature that helps users stay informed about what's trending in a way that
fosters engagement without overwhelming them. You need to decide what Community Pulse actually means...is
that trending posts, most active users, hottest topics, sentiment analysis, or something else entirely?

As the developer behind this feature, you'll need to address a few key challenges:

-   How do you surface insights without flooding users with too much data?

-   While Trend's platform is small today, it's growing. How will your design hold up when the platform

reaches 100,000+ users?

-   What technical architecture would you recommend implementing this feature? What constraints would be

most important?

-   What is the simplest version of this you can show to your team about your hypothesis?

Case Study 3: Vulnerability Rules

Scenario: Imagine you're building a platform where people feel safe enough to be emotionally vulnerable on the
internet. Vulnerability isn't something that scales easily. What feels intimate in a 1:1 conversation can feel
performative or unsafe in a large group. However, we want to design a system where millions of people can
engage in emotionally rich interactions at scale while maintaining a deep sense of psychological safety.

Task: Design a feature (or system) that enables Vulnerability at Scale. You'll need to solve problems around trust,
moderation, and pacing.

As the owner of this feature, you'll need to address some key challenges:

-   What fundamental principles would guide your design?

-   How could you prevent emotional exhaustion, bad actors, and unintentional harm?

-   What technical and UX guardrails could work to scale trust and psychological safety to millions of users?

-   What is the simplest thing you can build to demonstrate how to figure out if your solution is trending in the

right direction?
