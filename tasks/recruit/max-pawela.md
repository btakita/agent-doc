**On subcontracting as a government contractor's sub:**

Short answer: subcontracting portions of C2C work is generally legal and common in government contracting, but there are compliance considerations.

**What's allowed:**
- As a C2C subcontractor to ECCO Select, you're already one layer removed from the government contract. Further subcontracting work you receive is typically a matter of your agreement with ECCO, not directly governed by criminal statutes.
- Government prime contractors routinely use tiered subcontractor arrangements.

**What to watch for:**
- **Your subcontract terms:** ECCO's subcontractor agreement will likely have a "no further subcontracting without consent" clause. Violating that is a breach of contract (civil), not criminal.
- **Flow-down clauses:** Government contracts include FAR (Federal Acquisition Regulation) clauses that flow down to subs. Some require prior approval for further subcontracting (FAR 52.244-2). ECCO would need to manage this.
- **Criminal liability** would only arise in scenarios involving fraud (misrepresenting who performs the work), security clearance violations (if classified work), or false claims. Simply subcontracting work you're authorized to subcontract is not criminal.
- **Set-aside programs:** If the contract has small business/minority set-aside requirements, pass-through arrangements that circumvent those can trigger False Claims Act issues.

**Practical advice:**
- Read ECCO's subcontractor agreement carefully for assignment/delegation restrictions
- If you want to subcontract, ask Max directly whether that's permitted under their arrangement with WWT
- Be transparent about the arrangement ... trying to hide it is where legal risk escalates
