**California Storage/Garage Rental — Options for More Time**

The 30-day notice (Feb 12 → Mar 12) may or may not be legally sufficient. Here are Robert's options:

### If Robert has rented for 1+ year → 60-day notice required

California Civil Code § 1946.1 requires **60 days' notice** for month-to-month tenancies of 1 year or longer. If Robert has been renting this garage for over a year, the 30-day notice is **defective** and the deadline extends to **April 13**. This is the strongest argument if it applies.

### Harassment documentation

6+ contacts via text and phone to pressure Robert out could constitute harassment. Under Civil Code § 1940.2 (residential) and general harassment standards, Robert should **save all texts and document all calls** with dates/times. This is leverage in any negotiation or court proceeding.

### Negotiate an extension

Landlords often prefer a cooperative move-out over formal eviction. Robert can propose a specific date (e.g., March 26 or March 31) in writing. The landlord's alternative — unlawful detainer — takes 30-60+ days in CA courts.

### If Robert stays past March 12

The landlord **cannot** self-help evict (change locks, remove property, cut utilities). They must file an unlawful detainer action. That process (5-day summons + answer + trial) typically takes 3-6 weeks, effectively buying Robert more time — though it creates a court record.

### Key questions to determine the best strategy

1. **How long has Robert been renting?** (1+ year = 60-day notice required)
2. **Written lease or month-to-month?** (Fixed-term lease can't be terminated early without cause)
3. **Residential or commercial garage?** (Residential has stronger tenant protections)
4. **Was the notice properly served?** (Must be written, served per CCP § 1162)
